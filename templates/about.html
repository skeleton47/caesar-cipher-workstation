Mathematical foundations, cryptanalytic vulnerability profiles, and modern cryptographic standards.

§ 1. Formal Mathematical Model

The Caesar cipher is a monoalphabetic substitution cipher over the finite cyclic group $\mathbb{Z}_{26}$. Given an alphabet $\Sigma = \{A, B, \dots, Z\}$ indexed as integers $0 \dots 25$:

Encryption Transformation
C ≡ (P + K) mod 26

Where $P$ is plaintext index and $K \in [0, 25]$ is the shift key.

Decryption Transformation
P ≡ (C - K) mod 26

Equivalent to adding $(26 - K) \pmod{26}$.

§ 2. Cryptanalysis & Attack Vectors

Caesar cipher provides zero real-world confidentiality. It is susceptible to two direct, instantaneous attack models:

1. Exhaustive Key-Search Attack (Brute Force)

With only 25 non-trivial keys ($K = 1 \dots 25$), an adversary can test all keys in $\mathcal{O}(1)$ time. An operator can scan all 26 outputs in seconds.

2. Frequency Analysis (Chi-Squared $\chi^2$ Attack)

Monoalphabetic substitution preserves the unigram probability distribution. In English, the letter E appears with ~12.7% frequency, followed by T (~9.1%) and A (~8.2%). The highest peak in the ciphertext immediately exposes the shift.
