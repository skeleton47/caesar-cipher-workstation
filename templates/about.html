§ 1 Formal Mathematical Definition
Cyclic monoalphabetic substitution over the finite ring $\mathbb{Z}_{26}$

Let alphabet $\Sigma = \{A, B, \dots, Z\}$ be indexed as integer ring $\mathbb{Z}_{26} = \{0, 1, \dots, 25\}$. The Caesar cipher maps an arbitrary character $P_i \in \Sigma$ into ciphertext $C_i \in \Sigma$ parameterized by key $K \in \mathbb{Z}_{26}$:

Encryption Transformation
C ≡ (P + K) mod 26
Shift forward by key $K$ positions.
Decryption Transformation
P ≡ (C - K) mod 26
Equivalent to adding $(26 - K) \pmod{26}$.
§ 2 Cryptanalytic Vulnerability Profiles
Why Caesar offers zero confidentiality in modern threat models

1. Exhaustive Key-Search Attack (Brute Force)

The effective key space consists of only 25 non-trivial shifts ($|K| = 25$). Entropy is bounded by $\log_2(25) \approx 4.64\text{ bits}$. Any modern CPU completes exhaustive search in under 5 microseconds.

2. Unigram Frequency Invariance Attack

Because each character maps 1-to-1, natural language frequency distributions remain completely preserved. By locating the highest frequency character peak (in English, 'E' at ~12.7%), an analyst determines $K = (C_{\text{peak}} - 4) \pmod{26}$ instantly without testing other keys.
